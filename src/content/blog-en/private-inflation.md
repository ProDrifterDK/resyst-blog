Some spreadsheets look balanced and are not. Two columns, the same currency, the same date in the header. And yet one of those columns updates itself, every month, without anyone signing anything, while the other waits for someone to have the conversation.

In Chile this has a technical name and has been running for half a century. The UF is published daily; the UTM is set once a month and moves with the previous period's CPI (IPC). They are indexed units: they are not money, they are a rule for converting a debt into pesos on the day it has to be paid. They were born as an act of trust — a way of lending over thirty years in a country that had already watched its currency lose its meaning more than once — and in that origin there is something honest: if nobody knows what the peso will be worth, let us agree on a unit that does not depend on knowing.

Indexation is not a trap. It is a contract between two parties who distrust the immediate future and agree to tie themselves to the same clock. The problem appears when the clock is installed on only one of the two sides.

## Two Ways of Finding Out That Something Changed

I think of this as two ways of receiving a change of state. A system can subscribe to an event: when the value moves, someone notifies it, and the adjustment happens without human intervention. Or it can poll: someone has to remember to ask, and what they get is the value in force at the instant of the question, not the one that is owed.

An indexed obligation is subscribed. A nominal income polls. And it polls when there is a meeting, when the evaluation cycle allows it, when the person works up the nerve to raise it: three independent conditions, none of which is a calendar.

There lies the first asymmetry, and it is not one of rate. It is one of initiative. The index does not ask permission. The income does.

## Hedging Only One Leg

Finance has a precise image for this: if you hedge one leg of a position and leave the other exposed, you do not have a half-hedged position. You have a new bet, and probably one you did not choose.

Partial indexation does exactly that. On the side of the obligation there is a hedge against the loss of value of the currency: whatever happens to prices, the creditor is made whole. On the side of the income there is nothing equivalent. And a hedge on one half of the book is not prudence: it is a slow transfer, executed without a signature, month after month, with the accounting in order.

The uncomfortable part is that nobody decided. There was no meeting where a cut was approved. There is no identifiable culprit, no date, no document. Only two tables that update at different speeds, and a subtraction between them that grows on its own.

A loss with an author produces anger, and anger organizes itself. A loss by arithmetic produces fatigue. And fatigue files no claims.

## The Average Lives in No House

Then there is the problem of the measure, which comes before everything else.

The CPI (IPC) is a weighted average over a basket that represents the consumption of a population. No house has that basket. The basket of a particular house has weights that shift with the age of its inhabitants, with the city, with whether someone is ill, with whether there is a child of school age, with how much is spent on transport because work moved to another comuna, with how much of life has come to happen on a screen. Two households in the same country, the same month, the same official index, and real variations that bear no resemblance to each other.

When someone says inflation was four percent, they say something rigorously true about a statistical object and something approximate about anyone. The average lives in no house. And it is not indifference: it is built, precisely, so as not to describe anyone in particular. It is the same trap as the latency average, which can sit at forty milliseconds while the ninety-ninth percentile sits at nine hundred. The average describes the request that never happened.

There is one more step, and it is the one that changes the nature of the index. When an indexed unit is used to set the price of an obligation, the index stops measuring and starts legislating. It no longer reports how much prices rose: it decides how much you will pay. The measurement becomes decree in the same motion, and from that moment on, arguing with the index is arguing with the contract.

## What Can Be Done Without Public Policy

I have no policy to propose. I have a practice, and it seems to me the only honest one at this scale.

If a number is going to govern your life, be the one who publishes it. Keep your own basket, with your own weights, and update it with the same discipline with which what you are charged gets updated. Not to win the argument: to know when there is one. A plain, boring index of your own, with four or five lines and a date, turns a diffuse feeling of losing ground into a figure that can be looked at head-on.

And there is a second practice, a less comfortable one: counting the cost of asking. Every time an adjustment depends on you requesting it, the system is charging you something that appears on no spreadsheet — noticing, preparing the case, sustaining the conversation, hearing no. That cost is real, it is asymmetric, and it is almost never named. Naming it is half of correcting it.

## Why I Write This

A system where one quantity updates itself and the other waits for someone to have the courage (coraje) to ask is not a broken system. It is a system that works exactly as specified, and that produces a result nobody specified. That is the kind of failure that interests me most: the one that has no bug, only an absent owner.

National averages will keep being published, and it is good that they exist: they serve to compare countries, to set policy, to have a shared reference. But a reference is not a measure of you. Confusing the two is what allows a loss to feel like a personal problem of administration instead of what it is: a subtraction between two clocks that never agreed on the time.

Every house has its own index. The work is to write it before someone writes it for you.
