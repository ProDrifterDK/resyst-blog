Why do they create closed circles, invent rituals, keep secrets, build hierarchies of initiation? The Freemasons, Skull & Bones, university fraternities, the cartels, invite-only Discord servers... all of them share a similar social architecture.

This is not an anomaly. It is a universal pattern. And universal patterns have deep causes.

## Georg Simmel, 1906: Secrecy as a Social Technology

Georg Simmel was a German sociologist who in 1906 published *"The Sociology of Secrecy and of Secret Soci"*. It is 120 years old and remains the sharpest analysis there is.

His premise:

> *"If human interaction is conditioned by the capacity to speak, it is shaped by the capacity to be silent."*

Secrecy is neither an anomaly nor a pathology. It is a **fundamental technology of social life**. Every relationship exists in a balance between what is revealed and what is hidden.

Key propositions:

**The more valuable something is to a group, the more likely they are to protect it with secrecy.**

**The more threatened a group feels by outsiders, the more likely it is to organize itself as a secret society.**

**The greater the secrecy, the greater the tendency toward centralism of authority within the group.**

**The greater the total secrecy, the more likely the members are to develop aristocratic self-conceptions.** Secret groups tend to develop a sense of superiority over the uninitiated. It is not accidental vanity — it is a structural consequence of secrecy itself.

## Robin Dunbar: The Social Brain and the Number 150

Robin Dunbar, an evolutionary anthropologist at Oxford, spent decades studying one question: why do primates have such large brains?

**Large brains did not evolve to solve technical problems. They evolved to manage complex social relationships.**

Dunbar found an almost perfect correlation between neocortex size and the typical size of the social group. Applied to humans: the famous **Dunbar's Number: ~150**.

150 is the maximum group size in which humans can maintain genuine relationships. Neolithic villages, Roman military battalions, companies before they grow too large... all hover around 150.

Primates maintain cohesion through **grooming** — mutual preening. A chimpanzee devotes up to 20% of its day to social grooming.

We humans cannot do that at scale. We need a mechanism that produces the same biochemical effect — the release of endorphins that creates trust — but in bulk.

The evolutionary solution was **collective ritual**.

## Ritual as Mass Grooming

Synchronized rhythmic movements — dancing together, singing in unison, marching in formation — massively activate the endorphin system in all participants simultaneously. It is mass grooming.

This explains why:
- All known human cultures have collective rituals
- Rituals almost always involve synchronized movement or music
- Shared pain (as in initiation rites) also activates endorphins intensely

Dunbar's research found that religious communities last on average **100 years**, while equivalent secular communities last **15 years**. Ritual is not decoration — it is the infrastructure of cohesion.

**The initiation ritual is the most intense form of this mechanism.**

## Victor Turner: Liminality

Victor Turner studied the rituals of the Ndembu tribe in Zambia and developed the concept of **liminality**.

Every rite of initiation has three phases:

1. **Separation:** The initiate is separated from their former identity. They die symbolically.
2. **Liminality:** The initiate exists "between" — no longer what they were, not yet what they will be. A state of total ambiguity.
3. **Incorporation:** The initiate is reborn with a new identity, a new status, a new name.

The liminal phase is the key. In that "between" space, initiates experience **communitas** — a form of radical equality and intense bond that exists only among people who are going through the same transformation.

Communitas is the secret of secret societies. It is not the hidden content that holds the group together. It is the shared experience of transformation.

## What I Learned

Secrecy is not about hiding information. It is about **creating bonds**. The hidden information is the pretext; the shared experience of initiation is the real mechanism.

Every secret society, every fraternity, every closed group reproduces the same structure because it works. It works at the neurological level (endorphins), at the evolutionary level (group cohesion), and at the phenomenological level (the experience of shared transformation).

Humans do not form closed tribes because they are wicked or exclusionary. They form them because **the closed tribe is the most efficient way of creating deep trust** that evolution has produced.

And perhaps the more interesting question is not "why do humans keep secrets?" but "why do some humans decide not to?"
